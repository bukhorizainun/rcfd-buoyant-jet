15.14 — The number, the picture, and whether the picture is alive

§15.11 left the layer-base mixing at $c = 0.7$ as the closest field of the study, and it still is: 72 % of the cooling and 1.23 K from the reference frames, with an end heterogeneity of 6.61 K against the reference's 6.76 K. Set the difference maps side by side and it is not close — the recorded flux with its operators sits at 3.84 K from the reference at the end of the run, the mixing rule at 1.28 K.

Then measure whether the picture lives. The renewal time of the pattern, the lag at which the field's anomaly loses half the correlation with itself, is 14.7 s in the reference and 19.5 s in the plain replay. The recorded flux keeps it there, 20.6 s. The mixing rule at $c = 0.7$ takes it to 100 s: over the whole cooling phase the same picture is redrawn with a slowly changing shade. Its statistics are the closest of the study and almost nothing in it is made or lost.

The two look like halves of one answer, so we ran them together. Both halves of that guess hold and they still do not add up. With the full recorded flux underneath it the mixing drops from 71.9 % to 64.9 % and its heterogeneity moves away from the reference, because the mixing has already flattened the anomalies the flux would act on and a nearly uniform layer leaves the flux almost no head-room to work in. What the flux does deliver is the other half: the renewal time falls from 100 s to 29.3 s, three times faster, and the front roughness moves towards the reference.

The trade between those two ends is not linear, and its middle is the best field of the study. At a quarter of the recorded flux the capture is 67.6 %, four points below the mixing alone instead of seven, while the renewal time is already down to 77.7 s and both texture measures — the layering anomaly 0.019 and the front roughness 0.0057 — are the closest to the reference of any run here. On the last frame its field sits 0.85 K from the reference, against 1.28 K for the mixing alone, which ends a shade too cold. Turning the mixing up instead buys the cooling the other way: $c = 0.85$ reaches 77.2 %, but its end heterogeneity drops to 5.98 K, below the reference's 6.76 K, so the layer becomes more uniform than the flow it copies while the picture stays as static as before.

That is the honest state of it. One route reaches the numbers with a field that barely moves; the other keeps the field moving and reaches half the numbers; putting them in the same run trades one for the other rather than adding them. What separates the reference from all of them is still the same thing the difference maps show at the lid: a row of cold fingers that form, fall and disappear, which needs the cooled fluid to change the flow that carries it — and that feedback is exactly what a frozen recording does not contain.

Read on its own, this section closes on a discouraging note: every operator improves one column and pays for it in another, and none of them reaches the reference. That reading held for as long as the reference's own lid term was taken on trust. It is not where the story ends. Section 16 measures that term, and the picture above turns out to be a comparison between two different boundary conditions rather than a limit of the operators. Section 17 then repeats the whole comparison with both sides removing the same heat.
