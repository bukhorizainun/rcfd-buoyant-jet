13 — Turning the Dial: the h-Sweep

To make sure the sink and the balance accounting behave the way they should, we turned $h$ up well past its physical value and watched the cooling grow. Because the source is registered in the balance, the response should be clean and monotonic in $h$ — rising smoothly with no runaway — and it is:

The energy removed and $Q_\text{wall}$ both rise smoothly with $h$ — sub-linearly, as the cooling saturates — and in every case the global balance stays healthy: target and actual energy agree to within about $0.03\%$. That is the main check here: a local sink can be carried through the heat balance correctly, and the safety belt does not cancel it.

Two things to read off this. The model is well-behaved, with no runaway and no sign that the balance belt is cancelling the loss. And rCFD under-predicts at every $h$ where we have a CFD reference, both the physical $h = 8$ and the stronger $h = 100$; a much larger $h$ would be needed to make the reused database match reality, but that would be papering over the missing flow with an unphysical coefficient, not a real fix.
