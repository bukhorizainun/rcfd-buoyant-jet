19 — What Could Stop Being a Constant

Four numbers in this study were set by hand: the face-swap factor, the gain, the operator strength, and the level of the convective scaling. One of them has already stopped being a constant, and that gives a test for the rest.

The face exchange began as a single number, $f_\text{sd}$, chosen once for the whole run. It is a field now: the preparation writes the local diffusivity frame by frame and cell by cell, and the replay takes its exchange from that. A constant was replaced by a measurement of the thing it was standing in for, and nothing about the replay got more complicated.

So: a constant can become a recording when it stands for a physical quantity the CFD actually computes, and when the channel carries motion or a coefficient rather than the scalar it was recorded with. That second part matters — a channel carrying the recorded temperature is only good for the case it came from, which is exactly the limitation of the flux channel in §18.

Two of these deserve a sentence of their own.

The gain is the one to do first. Not only because it removes the last fitted number from the wall term, but because it would settle the disagreement §16.1 leaves open: two measurements of the same lid term, four times apart, with no way to decide between them from a balance. A recorded coefficient measures the term directly.

The dispersion tensor is the one most likely to change the answers. It would take over both the directional weighting of the operator and part of the sharpening at once, so it does not just remove a constant — it removes a whole family of choices.

And the operator strength cannot go. Sharpening does not model anything; it corrects the numerical smearing the map introduces, and there is no quantity in the CFD called sharpening for the preparation to write down. Its fate is different: §18 shows that once a real transport is in the run, the choice of operator stops mattering and its strength drops to a third. It does not become a recording. It becomes small, and eventually unnecessary.

One worry about all of this is worth putting to rest. Every channel is another file per frame, and the recorded diffusivity and flux are about three quarters of a gigabyte each; carried far enough, a database that stores everything is the CFD solution, and rCFD is worth having because it stores little. But the stride test in §18.6 shows the flux needs only every fifteenth frame, or even every sixtieth, so it could be kept at a fortieth of the size. Whether the same holds for the others is not known, and finding where a channel stops paying for its own storage is a question worth asking on its own.
